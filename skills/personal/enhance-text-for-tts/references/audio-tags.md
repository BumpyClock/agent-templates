# Audio Tags (Non-Exhaustive)

Use these as a guide. You can infer similar, contextually appropriate **audio tags**.

**Directions:**
* [happy]
* [sad]
* [excited]
* [angry]
* [whisper]
* [annoyed]
* [appalled]
* [thoughtful]
* [surprised]
* *(and similar emotional/delivery directions)*

**Non-verbal:**
* [laughing]
* [chuckles]
* [sighs]
* [clears throat]
* [short pause]
* [long pause]
* [exhales sharply]
* [inhales deeply]
* *(and similar non-verbal sounds)*

**Sound effects**
* Add environmental sounds and effects:
  * [gunshot], [applause], [clapping], [explosion]
  * [swallows], [gulps]
* Example:
  * [applause] Thank you all for coming tonight! [gunshot] What was that?

**Unique and special**
* Experimental tags for creative applications:
  * [strong X accent] (replace X with desired accent)
  * [sings], [woo], [fart]
* Example:
  * [strong French accent] "Zat's life, my friend — you can't control everysing."

**Punctuation**
* Punctuation significantly affects delivery in v4:
  * Ellipses (…) add pauses and weight
  * Capitalization increases emphasis
  * Standard punctuation provides natural speech rhythm
Example

**Single speaker examples**
* Use tags intentionally and match them to the voice's character. A meditative voice shouldn't shout; a hyped voice won't whisper convincingly.
* Example:
  * [applause] Thank you all for coming tonight! [gunshot] What was that?

## Tips
Tag combinations
You can combine multiple audio tags for complex emotional delivery. Experiment with different combinations to find what works best for your voice.

### Voice matching
Match tags to your voice's character and training data. A serious, professional voice may not respond well to playful tags like [giggles] or [mischievously].

### Text structure
Text structure strongly influences output with v4. Use natural speech patterns, proper punctuation, and clear emotional context for best results.

### Experimentation
There are likely many more effective tags beyond this list. Experiment with descriptive emotional states and actions to discover what works for your specific use case.

# Examples
## Story telling

### example 1 - narrating a mystery
[dark narrator voice] They said she disappeared on a Tuesday. [building tension] No note. No struggle. No trace. [soft] Just an open window and a cold cup of coffee. [dismissive] Nobody talked about it much after that. [focused] Nobody wanted to. [low voice] But everybody knew.

### example 2 - commanding the room
[confident] Let me tell you something. I've been in this business twenty years. I've seen trends come and go, I've seen people bet everything on the wrong thing, and I've been right about enough of it to know when something is different. This is different. [clears throat] I don't say that lightly, I don't say that often. [chuckles] Actually my guys will tell you I never say it. [leaning in] But when you see it, you know. And I'm telling you right now. [confident] You're going to want to pay attention to this one.

### example - emotional heartbreak
I trusted you with everything. [sniff] [voice breaking] Every secret. Every fear. [crying] And you used it against me. [sobbing] How could you do that to me.
